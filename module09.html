Adjusting Entries

In Module 08, you prepared the Unadjusted Trial Balance. The Debit and Credit totals may agree, but some account balances can still need updating because time has passed, resources have been used, revenue has been earned, or expenses have been incurred.

This module has several adjustment types—but they follow one central idea.At the end of the accounting period, ask: What has changed that the accounts do not yet fully show? Work through one situation at a time.

02 · The Period-End Checkpoint

Adjustments happen because the period has ended.

Most transactions are recorded as they occur. At the end of the accounting period, however, the accounts are reviewed before financial statements are prepared.

During January

Transactions happen and are recorded.

Cash is received and paid, services are rendered, supplies are purchased, and other transactions are journalized and posted.

JAN. 31, 2026
PERIOD END
Before statements

Review what January should report.

Has something been used, earned, incurred, or changed even though the accounts do not yet fully show it?

Where do I look for the “account balance”?Start with the General Ledger, where each account has its running or ending balance. The same balances are also listed in the Unadjusted Trial Balance prepared in Module 08. The adjustment updates those existing balances; it does not start the accounts over.

03 · Adjustment Map

Different situations. One period-end question.

Do not treat the adjustment types as unrelated entries. First identify what changed by the end of the period.

DEFERRAL

Prepaid Expense

Cash was paid earlier. Part of the asset has now been used.

DEFERRAL

Unearned Revenue

ACCRUAL

ESTIMATE / ALLOCATION

Depreciation

Part of a long-term asset's depreciable cost is allocated to the current period.

ESTIMATE

Part of Accounts Receivable is estimated to be uncollectible.

Keep this boundary in mind.We are learning basic period-end adjustments. Each example will begin with a situation or transaction, then move to the amount that belongs to the current period.
Illustration Business

Meet PRIME Vulcanizing

PRIME Vulcanizing is an established service business that has already been operating for some time. Its primary operations include vulcanizing, tire repair, and related services. Its regular transactions have already been journalized and posted during the accounting period.

PRIME also owns a small commercial unit beside its shop that it rents to another business, giving it an alternative source of income apart from its normal service operations.

It is now January 31, 2026. Before financial statements are prepared, PRIME reviews its accounts and additional period-end information to determine whether any balances still need to be updated.

We are not starting PRIME's accounting records from the beginning.Earlier transactions have already been recorded. When an earlier transaction helps explain an adjustment, we will briefly look back at the original entry, then determine what must be updated at January 31.

06 · Accrual · Accrued Expenses

The expense belongs to this period even if payment comes later.

Accrued expenses are expenses already incurred but not yet paid or fully recorded.

Period-end information · January 31, 2026

PRIME Vulcanizing's employees have already worked the final three days of January. Salaries attributable to those days amount to ₱900 per day and will be paid on February 2.

January 29–31

Employees perform the work.

The business has already received the employees' service. The expense belongs to January.

JAN. 31
ADJUST

Cash will be paid later.

The future payment date does not move January's expense into February.

07 · Accrued Interest

A note has terms—and interest can accumulate before payment.

A note payable states a principal amount and may carry an annual interest rate. Even when interest is paid later, part of it may already belong to the current period.

PRIME Vulcanizing borrowed ₱120,000 and issued a 90-day, 12% note payable. The principal and interest will be paid at maturity.

08 · Accrual · Accrued Income

Income can be earned even when cash has not yet been received.

PRIME's main source of income is vulcanizing, tire repair, and related services. An established business may also earn income from other sources.

Rental arrangement · January 2026

PRIME owns a small commercial unit beside its shop and rents it to a motorcycle-parts retailer for ₱6,000 per month. Rent for each month is payable on the 5th day of the following month.

By January 31, the tenant has already occupied the unit for the entire month, but the January rent will not be collected until February 5.

During January

The tenant uses the commercial unit.

PRIME has already earned ₱6,000 Rental Income for January.

JAN. 31
PERIOD END
February 5

Cash is collected later.

The collection occurs in the next period, but the income belongs to January.

Does PRIME have income for January even though the tenant has not yet paid?Yes. The tenant has already occupied the unit for January. PRIME has therefore earned the ₱6,000 Rental Income, even though cash will be collected in February.
What has already happened?

Rental income was earned.

January should report Rental Income of ₱6,000.

What has not happened yet?

Cash has not been collected.

January should also report Rent Receivable of ₱6,000.

Adjusting entry · January 31, 2026
Rent Receivable₱6,000
Rental Income₱6,000
To record rental income earned but not yet collected
The accrued-income patternIncome earned now → Cash received later. The period-end adjustment recognizes both the income already earned and the related receivable.
Connect this with Accounts Receivable.When PRIME performs its normal vulcanizing or repair services on account, the receivable and Service Revenue are ordinarily recorded when the transaction occurs. Here, the January rent has already been earned but remains unrecorded at period-end, so an adjusting entry is needed. Rent Receivable makes the source of the receivable clear.
Another example

Accrued income can also arise from PRIME's normal operations.

Suppose PRIME completes repair services for a regular fleet customer near the end of January, but the service will not be billed until February. If the earned amount has not yet been recorded, the receivable and Service Revenue would need to be accrued at January 31.

09 · Depreciation

Long-term assets help the business for more than one period.

Depreciation expense allocates the depreciable cost of a plant asset over its estimated useful life. For now, we will use straight-line depreciation, the simplest starting method.

Cost

The acquisition cost of the depreciable asset.

Salvage Value

The estimated value expected at the end of the asset's useful life.

Estimated Useful Life

The estimated period over which the asset is expected to be useful to the business.

Other depreciation methods exist.Examples include declining-balance and units-of-production methods. This module focuses on straight-line depreciation so you can first understand the period-end adjustment.

10 · Doubtful Accounts

Not every receivable may be collected.

At period-end, a business may estimate that part of its Accounts Receivable will be uncollectible. The estimate is recorded without reducing Accounts Receivable directly.

Period-end information · January 31, 2026

Because PRIME has been operating for some time, some customers have received vulcanizing and repair services on account. At January 31, Accounts Receivable has a ₱100,000 balance. Based on experience, 8% is estimated to be uncollectible.

Does this mean PRIME already knows exactly which customer will not pay?No. This is an estimate of the receivables that may not be collected.

11 · Required Balance vs Adjustment

What if the account already has a balance?

The amount a problem says should be in an account at period-end may be the required ending balance—not automatically the amount of the adjusting entry.

What if...

PRIME still requires an ₱8,000 credit balance in Allowance for Doubtful Accounts at January 31. But because the business has been operating for some time, the General Ledger already shows a ₱1,000 credit balance in Allowance for Doubtful Accounts before adjustment.

Do we record another ₱8,000?No. First compare what is already there with what is required at period-end.

13 · After the Adjusting Entries

What happens to the adjustments?

Preparing an adjusting entry is not the end of the process. Like the journal entries you learned earlier, an adjusting entry must move forward into the ledger so that the affected account balances are updated.

1 · Adjusting EntryRecord the period-end adjustment in the General Journal.
2 · Post to the LedgerPost the Debit and Credit to the accounts affected by the adjustment.
3 · Updated BalancesThe adjustment becomes part of each account's existing balance.
4 · Adjusted Trial BalanceList the updated account balances and test Debit = Credit again.
Nothing is being started over.The adjustment is added to what is already in the account. The existing ledger balance and the adjustment together determine the adjusted balance.
Example from PRIME Vulcanizing

Recall the accrued-salaries adjustment.

Salaries Expense₱2,700
Salaries Payable₱2,700
To record salaries already incurred but not yet paid
Then post it

The affected accounts are updated.

Salaries Expense increases by ₱2,700. Whatever balance it already had is updated by the adjustment.

Salaries Payable increases by ₱2,700 because the amount is owed at period-end.

Do we simply add one total adjustment to the Unadjusted Trial Balance?No. Each adjusting entry is first posted to the individual ledger accounts it affects. After all adjustments are posted, the resulting account balances are used to prepare the Adjusted Trial Balance.

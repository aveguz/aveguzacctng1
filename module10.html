03 · The Worksheet

A bridge between the trial balance and financial statements

A worksheet, or working paper, is a columnar sheet used to organize accounting information before the financial statements are prepared.

ORGANIZE

Bring information together

Place account balances and adjustments in one organized working paper.

REVIEW

Trace the balances

See how period-end adjustments affect individual accounts.

PREPARE

Move toward statements

Organize adjusted balances according to where they will be reported.

A worksheet is not a formal financial statement.It is an optional working paper. Financial statements can still be prepared using the trial balance and adjusting information without preparing a worksheet.

06 · Step 1

Start with what PRIME already has.

The account balances are transferred to the Trial Balance columns. They are not recomputed simply because we are preparing a worksheet.

First checkpointTotal Trial Balance Debits must equal total Trial Balance Credits.
07 · Step 2

Place each adjustment beside the account it affects.

Accounts needed by an adjusting entry can be added even if they did not carry an unadjusted balance.

Notice the new accounts.Rent Receivable, Salaries Payable, Interest Payable, Accumulated Depreciation, and adjustment-related expense accounts may enter the worksheet because period-end information requires them.
Second checkpointTotal Adjustment Debits must equal total Adjustment Credits.

09 · Step 4

Now update every affected account.

The first six numeric columns now work together: Trial Balance + Adjustments → Adjusted Trial Balance.

Adjusted Trial Balance checkpointAfter all adjustments are incorporated, total adjusted Debits and Credits should agree. Equality tests arithmetic equality; it does not guarantee that every accounting decision was correct.

11 · Steps 5–6

Extend the adjusted balances.

Extend means transfer the adjusted balance to the appropriate worksheet column. It does not mean journalize the account again.

Income Statement

Revenue and income retain their Credit position. Expenses retain their Debit position.

Balance Sheet

Assets, liabilities, equity, contra-assets, and withdrawals retain the appropriate Debit or Credit position.

12 · Step 7

The statement columns will not necessarily balance yet.

Foot, or total, the Income Statement and Balance Sheet columns before entering net income or net loss.

Is something wrong because the last columns do not yet agree?Not necessarily. At this stage, the difference represents net income or net loss.

14 · What If?

When expenses exceed income

PRIME does not have a net loss in this illustration, so consider this rule separately.

Think about equity.Net income increases owner's equity; net loss reduces owner's equity. That helps explain the Balance Sheet-column placement.
15 · Completed Worksheet

Now the 10-column worksheet comes together.

After net income is entered, total the Income Statement and Balance Sheet columns again. Equal totals are double-ruled to indicate completion.

Reminder when there is Net IncomeEnter Net Income on the Debit side of the Income Statement columns to balance them, and on the Credit side of the Balance Sheet columns because net income increases owner's equity. Rule the Net Income line; double-rule the final equal totals.
The worksheet is complete.Every account can now be traced from its unadjusted balance, through any adjustment, to its adjusted balance and appropriate statement column.
